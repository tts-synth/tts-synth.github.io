Updated CLS Mappings for the paper:: Kinship in Speech : Leveraging Linguistic Relatedness for Zero-Shot TTS in Indian Languages

The green coloured labels are updated in the below CLS mappings.

  • The chillu characters in the Malayalam script (‘ൺ’, ‘ൻ’, ‘ർ’, ‘ൽ’, ‘ൾ’) are unique consonant forms that do not have an inherent vowel and often modify adjacent consonants to produce distinct phonetic sounds. Based on expert linguistic analysis and observations of the generated speech output, these characters were mapped to their closest phonetic equivalents:

    • ‘ൺ’ corresponds to the sound ‘nx’, which represents a retroflex nasal similar to ‘ണ’ in ‘പണം’ (panam - money). Experts noted that this sound is characterized by a nasal closure with retroflex articulation, aligning closely with the ‘nx’ sound.
    • ‘ൻ’ is mapped to ‘n’, as seen in ‘ന’ in ‘വനം’ (vanam - forest). This follows the alveolar nasal pronunciation commonly found in Malayalam phonology.
    • ‘ൽ’ and ‘ൾ’ correspond to ‘l’ and ‘lx’, respectively. These represent lateral consonants, as observed in words like ‘മലയാളം’ (Malayalam). Experts identified that ‘ൽ’ produces a standard lateral sound, while ‘ൾ’ has a more retroflex quality, leading to its mapping with ‘lx’.
    • ‘ർ’ closely maps to the sound ‘r’, as found in ‘ര’ in ‘കരി’ (kari - charcoal). Malayalam’s ‘ർ’ is typically an alveolar tap or trill, aligning with the ‘r’ sound in phonetic transcriptions.
  • For Bengali, experts compared the phonetic realization of Devanagari ‘व’ (pronounced as ‘va’) and determined that its closest equivalent in Bengali is ‘b’ (pronounced as ‘bo’ or ‘bô’). In Bengali phonology, ‘v’ and ‘b’ sounds often overlap, with ‘व’ being pronounced closer to ‘bô’ in many dialects. This expert-guided mapping ensures consistency in pronunciation across different language systems.
